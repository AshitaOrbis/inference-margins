Frontier Inference Margins

An interactive cost model for one token of a frontier LLM, and the serving margin it implies — of each dollar of modeled token billings, how much is left after the modeled direct cost of serving them. (The full name, used once in the methods box, is unit direct-serving contribution margin; it is a list-price figure only when the batch and discount sliders are 0%, and it is not a company gross margin.)

Where the numbers stand for Claude Opus. The calculator opens on the GPT-5.6 Pro estimate's own settings unless you choose another default: its author states 83.1% at list price, worked from this calculator's reading of 2026-08-07, and the calculator reads about 82% at the same settings today — its card explains the gap. This page's own planning baseline — planning rents, 50% utilization, the Reference mix, no assumed efficiency lead — reads about 58% at effective price, and about 68% with the page's adopted 3-month algorithmic lead. A second estimate, Fable 5's, reads ≈77% at effective price. The 90–95% figure repeated in the discourse needs assumptions beyond this page's planning baseline. Every number here is a modeled reading from public sources and labeled assumptions; nothing is any provider's actual ledger.

Prices and inputs are current as of July 15, 2026 unless a different effective date is shown. Each accelerator's speed was set its own way and says so on its row (its calibration class): TPU v7 is an analyst-set bridge from the same platform; Trainium 2/3 carry a joint coefficient fitted across other hardware families, with no matched serving measurement of their own; Rubin is a projection from its announced shape only. Where the model does not fit an accelerator's memory at the assumed batch, the row shows a smaller batch or no number. Every slider documents its sources. Adjust the assumptions.

What this calculator does — and deliberately does not — model (read before quoting numbers)
  • Superseded readings (history). Before the August 2026 repair this page's evidence-informed blend read ≈37% and its declared-topology blend ≈48%, on defaults the fourth adversarial review found defective (per-device batch/weight identity, b = 4 Trainium operating points, cross-platform throughput coefficients, no explicit multi-token prediction (MTP), several low or analyst-set rents). Those readings are superseded by the repair and are preserved in git history. They are not selectable, and this page does not present them as a coherent public-market-rent case or as a central estimate of any provider's economics.
  • The metric. Serving margin = 1 − serving cost ÷ effective price, for the selected traffic mix. Its full technical name, said here once, is unit direct-serving contribution margin. Effective price is the list price after the selected cache-read price, batch share and negotiated discount — a modeled figure, not observed provider revenue. With batch share and discount at 0% it is simply list price; the provider cards' replays are computed that way and the planning baseline is not. Serving cost counts accelerator time — rented or owned, at the selected cost basis — the serving software's throughput, and paid idle capacity, which is charged to the tokens served by dividing by utilization. It does not separately model support, research and development, sales, revenue shares, free traffic, or unbilled retries and storage. It is neither a company's cost of revenue (COGS) nor a company gross margin.
  • Which kind of price you assume moves the answer by tens of points. It is not the widest lever on this page and this sentence no longer claims it is: on the xAI card below, sweeping utilization across the range this page offers moves the same reading further still — 99 points against the valuation choice's 72. It is simply the lever most often left unstated when a margin figure is quoted. On the same Grok 4.5 fleet and the same workload, the calculator reads ~91% if the accelerator-hour is valued at short-run cash cost, ~63% at full-cycle owned cost, and ~19% at what a customer pays for that hour. Those are three answers to three different questions, not a disagreement — which is why a figure from one cost basis cannot be set beside a figure from another.
  • Each accelerator's speed was set its own way, and no one number transfers between them. Fitted rows reproduce their declared calibration points; H100/H200 use an explicit H800-family transfer; TPU v7 is an analyst-set same-platform bridge; Trainium uses an out-of-family joint coefficient without a matched serving anchor; GB300 is analyst-set at an assumed operating point; Rubin is projection-only. The live roofline then resolves compute, high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and interconnect terms at the selected operating point. A single cross-platform scalar previously failed (mean error 37%), and the physically informed follow-up also failed its whole-platform gate (methods note).
  • Three kinds of price, and where electricity is counted. every price on this page is typed to one of exactly three cost bases — on-demand rent (published rate cards; the China public-cloud preset), planning rent (the registered low- and committed-term rates this page plans on, and its default), and owned cost, also called total cost of ownership (TCO), built from purchase price, power, the datacenter and operations. The page never silently substitutes one basis for another inside one number, and the suite fails the build if it does; a fleet declared with some legs rented and some owned is allowed, and says so on the leg. The three valuation replays on the xAI card (cash cost, owned cost, opportunity cost) are a separate axis layered on a rent basis, not a fourth basis. Electricity dollars are explicit only under owned cost; under rent bases they are embedded in the rent — decomposing rent without TCO assumptions would be fabrication, so rent lenses show only the implied physical energy (info-only). Serving energy (Wh per M tokens) is computed for every leg and every lens as operating power × power usage effectiveness (PUE) ÷ achieved throughput at the engine's own operating point. Three declared conventions: (1) operating power is the thermal design power (TDP) proxy — no per-accelerator operating-power measurement is public (a typed boardPowerW override hook exists, currently null everywhere); (2) no utilization divisor — the dollar path allocates paid idle to served tokens, while the Wh figure deliberately does not: it is the physical intensity at the operating point, so it diverges from the electricity-$ path, which does divide by utilization. Idle boards do draw power; this page does not model that draw, so the Wh figure is an operating-point intensity and not a fleet-average one; (3) cache reads are charged the same analyst-set 5%-of-prefill fraction the cost side uses, as a proxy. Scope is accelerator × PUE only: the one full-stack telemetry anchor in this corpus (accelerators ≈58% of per-prompt energy, a 1.72× stack overhead) implies a full-stack figure ≈1.4× above this page's number at the default PUE 1.25.
  • Cache writes: billable write premiums (OpenAI 1.25×; Anthropic 1.25×/2× by TTL) are modeled revenue-side via the cache-write sliders, default off; the serving-cost side is unchanged because prefill produces the keys and values (KV) regardless. Cache storage, transfer, retention, replication and reads-per-write are unmodeled and may be material.
  • Context length is now modeled, not free-form: the traffic-mix profile's fixed input/output lengths feed the decode and prefill roofline directly (KV footprint, fabric traffic, HBM/compute binding terms, feasibility capping) — it is not a slider you set independently, but it is no longer ignored. Still not modeled: time-to-first-token and time-per-output-token (TTFT/TPOT) latency targets, KV-cache lifecycle and storage billing, speculative-decoding acceptance, unbilled retries/safety reruns (a served-vs-billed overhead you can approximate with the stack-efficiency slider), and free traffic.
  • Traffic-mix axis (v2.1.2): the Traffic mix selector owns the I/O ratio and cache-hit share only — profiles carry provenance-honest names (a disclosure, a named deployment, a stated convention; no generic "chat/coding" archetypes). SLA/interactivity, service class and cache lifecycle remain modeled elsewhere; the full workload ontology is future work. The serving-side reuse share and the billable cached-input share are DIFFERENT observables; by default the calculator assumes them equal (a labeled assumption you can now break with the "Billable cached-input share" slider). The assumption is material: on the Opus flagship scenario at the public-evidence reference (algorithmic lead 0 months), billable cached share 0% / =serving / 95% spans 75.3% / 58.4% / 31.1% margin — the lead-adjusted baseline carries the adopted 3-month algorithmic lead, so its levels sit higher while the spread stays the same shape. Replay presets lock their traffic (it is part of the published operating point); the hero's scenario-preset span holds the selected traffic fixed and therefore excludes traffic-mix uncertainty.
  • Most of the modeled cost is on the input side, and it rides on one borrowed number. (1) a cache-read serving cost of 5% of fresh prefill — analyst-set, unobserved, and distinct from the published 10% cache-read price. It is load-bearing: at the reference 15:1 mix and 60% cache hits the executed cost identity is C = C_out + 15 × (0.40 + 0.60 × 0.05) × C_in = C_out + 6.45 × C_in, putting 72.45% of modeled direct cost on the input side at the planning baseline, all of it riding on a single universal H800 prefill transfer (an executed ±50% input-cost sensitivity moves the headline by ±15.1 points on the planning baseline; the lead-adjusted baseline gives a smaller move). The whole chain is reconstructed in one place, component by component with each source and label, together with the cache-work boundary the fresh-prefill anchor assumes and why that boundary is worth 6.4%: where the input-side cost comes from. (2) a peak KV/recurrent-state charge of ≈0.562 GB per live sequence at the reference terminal context length (16,000 tokens) — computed from the architecture registry and the traffic profile, not hand-set, and distinct from the 15,500-token representative decode position. Both were recovered by adversarial review of the cost arithmetic; stating and enforcing them is the fix.
  • Which cells are scenario-only: the Trainium2/Trainium3 throughput coefficients (no matched serving anchor exists for either part), the Trainium3 rent (no public rate), the GB300 batch and η (an assumed operating point — the row is analyst-set, not fitted), the H100/H200 topology carry, the fleet weights, the 2.5 T/300 B geometry, the prefill costs, the 50% utilization floor, the 15:1 / 60% / 15% / 5% commercial mix, the loaded-byte overhead, ancillary cost, and MTP acceptance. Each is a declared choice this page can defend as a scenario and cannot defend as a measurement; each names what disclosure would upgrade it.
  • Rental class: the same chip spans up to 10× between internet-data-centre (IDC) annual leases and hyperscaler on-demand (H20: ¥5–72/card-hr). China rows default to annual-commit rates; the cost multiplier is the dial.
  • Algorithmic lead — a labeled scenario prior, not a measurement. the "Algorithmic lead" slider asks how many months a lab's private serving stack is ahead of published open practice, in residual, non-hardware, accelerator-portable efficiency this page does not otherwise model. It converts through E = rate^(months/12) at a ratified 3×/yr (halving ≈7.57 months — Gundlach et al., arXiv:2511.23455, MIT FutureTech, with Epoch AI data), and modeled cost-out divides by E in every serving phase. The per-lab defaults are an adopted scenario prior: Anthropic / OpenAI / Google +3 months, DeepSeek +1, the other frontier Chinese labs 0, and any lab with no adopted prior 0 and labeled unassessed. Validity conditions: only residual gains belong here — hardware, quantization, batching and caching are modeled by their own controls, and representing them here as well counts them twice. Axes never conflate: capability lag (~4 months, Epoch-measured) is a different axis and is never this slider; on the efficiency axis the open Chinese labs define the published-practice zero, which is why their prior is 0 rather than negative. Refused rates: the published token-price series (9×–900×/yr — Epoch's price index, a16z's 10×, the AI Index's 280×) measure published prices, not serving efficiency; this slider never uses them. Under a published-operating-point replay the slider is inert and locked at 0 — the lab's actual efficiency is already inside the replay, so months on top would double-count.
  • Family multipliers — a broad stress lever, sourced by nothing. one ±% efficiency multiplier per hardware family (NVIDIA / TPU / Trainium / Ascend) multiplies achieved throughput for every card in that family, in every serving phase, in any fleet. It answers "suppose this family's real serving efficiency is N% off our calibration" and is a declared stress, never a measurement. It does not change declared batch, precision tuples, capacity or feasibility. A custom-fleet leg rides its donor's family; a renamed leg tagged unclassified is exempt and says so on the leg.
  • The interlock, and why the stack multiplier was redefined. the family multipliers and the algorithmic-lead prior are both broad, unspecified efficiency assumptions, so composing them silently would count the same improvement twice. Editing either group locks the other with the reason stated inline; because the ratified lead defaults start nonzero, the first family edit replaces the prior — the lead is set to 0 and locked, loudly, so every headline move stays attributable. Unlocking is always available, always deliberate, and leaves a persistent stacking banner. Explicitly-modeled levers — serving precision, serving regime, the cache controls — are never locked by this machinery; where they overlap the prior you get a non-blocking warning instead. In the same spirit, serving-stack efficiency is now defined as measured stack composition relative to published open practice: its 1.0 tick names the same referent as the lead slider's zero, and the old "frontier lab (assumed)" label is retired, because a frontier assumption is precisely what the lead slider now represents defensibly (1.25× ≈ +2.4 months at 3×/yr). Setting both above their baselines fires an overlap warning; the §10 dive replays keep their anchored stack values unchanged.
  • Speculative-decode credit. A scenario lever, off by default, that multiplies modelled decode throughput only — prefill has no autoregression to speculate on. It models no acceptance rate, draft cost or verification overhead: it is a credit you declare, not a mechanism this page runs. It is available only from the "no MTP/disagg" stack setting (0.7). 0.7 is the only point on this scale whose MTP-free meaning is portable to any scenario. Other settings carry no portable claim either way — a published replay may document its own stack in detail, but that documents that operating point, not the scale. Crediting speculative decoding from any other setting could therefore count part of the same improvement twice, and this page refuses rather than guess. The stack setting is not what proves a leg speculation-free, and an MTP-free setting would not prove it — speculative decoding does not require MTP. That work is done per leg: every leg carries a typed baseline status, and only legs whose own baseline is documented to exclude speculative uplift are creditable at all. The setting gates the scale; the per-leg status gates the leg. What that costs you: the 0.7 setting also removes disaggregation, so this page cannot express "disaggregation without speculative decoding" — those fleets are under-credited here rather than risk double-counting, which is the direction this page prefers to be wrong in. Energy follows the throughput multiplier by construction of this model — speculative decoding also burns draft-and-verify energy this page does not measure. Legs whose deployed efficiency absorbs speculation, and legs whose status this page cannot establish, are exempt and say so.
  • Implementation & regression consistency — not empirical validation: every §10 card's headline regenerates from the "§10 dive replay" perspective, and a deterministic release suite (several hundred assertions, published at /tests/ together with the roofline diagnostic) gates every deploy. These tests prove the code is stable and reproduces its own authored targets — they do not validate the economic inputs: out-of-sample flagship validations, calibrated coverage tests, and per-provider production-telemetry matches are all zero, and the single cross-platform transfer test that was run failed (§3).

2a · DeepSeek's serving disclosure — the one primary source in the field

On March 1, 2025 (Open Source Week "Day 6"), DeepSeek published actual production serving statistics for V3/R1 on H800 clusters: 73.7k input / 14.8k output tokens per second per 8-GPU H800 node, 56.3% input cache-hit rate, ~$87,072/day GPU cost (at $2/H800-hr) against $562,027/day theoretical revenue at R1 list prices — the famous "545% cost-profit ratio," which is a markup; as a margin it is 84.5% (a distinction TeorTaxes himself insisted on). 3 · The cost model and its calibration

Expand this sectionCollapse this section

The calculator prices a token with one identity. The cost of a million output tokens is the price of an accelerator-hour, divided by how many tokens that accelerator produces in an hour, divided again by utilization so that paid idle time lands on the tokens actually served:

output $ per million tokens = ($ per accelerator-hour ÷ 3,600 ÷ tokens per second per accelerator) × 10⁶ ÷ utilization

The tokens-per-second figure is where the modeling is. For decode it is the roofline: the slowest of the accelerator's compute, its memory bandwidth (high-bandwidth memory, HBM) and its interconnect sets the ceiling, and an efficiency factor (η) set per accelerator says how much of that ceiling is reached. The selected serving regime fixes each leg's batch; if the model and its working memory do not fit the accelerator at that batch, the batch is capped or the leg is marked infeasible. The traffic mix fixes the output length and therefore the context length; the precision setting fixes, per leg, the FLOPS rate and the byte widths the roofline is computed from. Two settings are fixed on every rendered path — one token position per step (q = 1) and one accepted token per step (a = 1); the roofline note defines them. Prefill has its own compute-and-interconnect roofline, with one reviewed prefill calibration applied to every accelerator. A cache read costs a selected fraction of a fresh prefill. The traffic mix blends those costs, and the same mix prices revenue at list minus the cache, batch and negotiated discounts.

These are calibration points, not a validated predictive model. Each leg's η carries a calibration class that says how it was set, and only a coefficient that reproduces a matched observation is labeled fitted. Five legs are borrowed, each in its own way: the H100 and H200 are explicit transfers within the H800 family; TPU v7 is an analyst-set bridge from the same platform in aggregate form; Trainium2 and Trainium3 carry an out-of-family joint coefficient, with no matched serving measurement of their own. The GB300 is analyst-set at an assumed operating point. Rubin is a projection only. The H20 and Ascend observations informed the frozen joint calibration, but their live per-leg coefficients are separate source-informed neutral choices that do not reproduce those observations, so they are not fitted either.

Two cautions apply to all of it. Reproducing a calibration point is an identity, not a validation. And every anchored observation is a model of at most about 50B active parameters, while the flagship scenarios assume 120–300B active. The obvious test was run and published: the previous single-scalar transfer test failed (mean error 37%, worst 59%), and the physically informed follow-up still failed the whole-platform gate (worst −40%). The conclusion: conditional, not blanket. A 90–95% serving margin at list price needs assumptions beyond this page's planning baseline.

On the planning baseline — the page-adopted 2.5T Opus, planning rents, algorithmic lead 0 months, family multipliers 1.0× — the modeled serving margin is about 58% at effective price. Across the page's compatible price presets that scenario spans about 58% to 83%. The calculator's lead-adjusted baseline adds the adopted 3-month algorithmic lead — a labeled assumption, not a measurement — and reads about 68% (about 68–87% across the same presets). Every calculator figure in this section is the planning baseline unless it says otherwise; 90–95% is the external claim under examination, not a reading of this page.

Two limits keep this from being a central or verified estimate. First, the fleet is assumed, and three different coverages have to be kept apart. All 7 of 7 declared legs are memory-feasible under the page's planning convention. Since 2026-09-10 all 7 of 7 also carry a planning accelerator-hour price. GB200, GB300 and Trainium3 still have no published rate of the low/committed planning class, and the three registry rows that price them are registered provisional and say so in their own source lines; before that adoption those three legs were excluded and this reading was computed over about 52% of declared fleet weight, which moved it from about 51% to about 58%. Since 2026-09-20 the reading above is computed over 5 of the 7, not all seven: the two Trainium legs are withdrawn from the default on evidence grounds — their operating point declares batch replica-global while this engine consumes it per chip, an open unit question worth about 15×, and this page's own hardware ledger already said that coefficient cannot support a central Trainium margin. Their declared 8% and 17% renormalize over the remaining five, and that renormalization is stated wherever the number is. Nothing is deleted: both rows keep their price, their dossiers and their scenarios, and the declared seven-leg topology is still selectable beside this one. One sourced coefficient that pins the batch/replica/accelerator identity puts them back. And which accelerators actually serve Opus, in what shares — the serving deployment — remains unverified. Second, and more serious, the model's own form is unresolved: on the planning baseline, holding each leg's efficiency factor (η) fixed while re-expressing the older traffic assumption produces a 47.61–61.30% span — an open calibration debt of 13.69 points — and the Trainium batch interpretation, which may make those legs ~15× wrong, is the reason they are now withdrawn from the default rather than carried inside it under a caveat.
